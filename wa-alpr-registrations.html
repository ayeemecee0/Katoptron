Washington state law: requirements and dates

These are statutory requirements under Washington's 2026 ALPR law, effective March 30, 2026. They are not findings that any listed agency uses Flock or complies with, or violates, the law. Individual agency policies linked below have not been independently read and evaluated for these criteria.

Retention: general 21-day limit

RCW 10.130.040 generally prohibits retaining covered ALPR records longer than 21 days, with specific exceptions including qualifying court process, parking enforcement, traffic studies, commercial vehicle enforcement and certain evidentiary uses. This is not an unconditional 21-day rule for every ALPR record.

Read RCW 10.130.040 (retention and exceptions)

Registration: required but self-certified

RCW 10.130.030 requires agencies using covered ALPR systems to register and certify policy and training safeguards. The state's registration list reflects submitted certifications, not Attorney General verification of the statements. Some registered systems may be for parking, traffic or university use rather than Flock police cameras.

Read RCW 10.130.030 (operation and registration)

Future policy and reporting deadlines

RCW 10.130.070 directs the Attorney General to publish model policies by July 1, 2027. By December 1, 2027, covered agencies must submit policy documentation or explain departures and provide annual public ALPR reporting, including disclosed sharing entities, policy changes, audits and camera locations. For policy modifications or repeals after December 1, 2026, a 60-day Attorney General notice provision applies. These deadlines must not be portrayed as already missed in October 2026.

Read RCW 10.130.070 (policies and reporting dates)
